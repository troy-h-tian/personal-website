Introduction

Troy Tian

CS & Asian American Studies  ·  UCLA  ·  AI Safety & Policy

I'm an undergraduate at UCLA studying Computer Science with a minor in Asian American Studies. My work sits at the intersection of machine learning, AI safety, and policy — I'm interested in how alignment failures propagate through systems, how institutions respond to new technology, and the political and cultural forces that shape both.

On the technical side, I've done ML/RL research, built PPO-based reinforcement learning agents, and am currently working on a research project examining how LLM alignment mechanisms degrade epistemic diversity in political framing. On the policy side, I think carefully about AI governance, liability frameworks, and the geopolitics of AI development.

I also write — about Chinese American history, Chinese political history, immigration regimes in Asia, and whatever else I find worth thinking through carefully.

AI Liability Caps: A Price-Anderson Analysis

Martingale Evaluation of Human–LLM Interaction

A proposed first-step experiment for detecting sycophancy and epistemic drift in LLM interactions using a martingale-based scoring system — framed as a stochastic decision process.

04

Algorithmic Monocultures in LLM Political Framing

URC Sciences research proposal: do LLM alignment techniques systematically degrade epistemic diversity? A research design using adversarial prompts, LIME/SHAP interpretability, and latent variable decomposition.

05

The Semantic Space Between: American Chinese Cuisine

An AP English senior project tracing how the American Chinese food industry has mirrored, shaped, and been shaped by the Chinese American immigrant experience — from the Gold Rush to Panda Express.
